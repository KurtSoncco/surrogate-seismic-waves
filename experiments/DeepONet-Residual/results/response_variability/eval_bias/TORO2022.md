## Reading

- **1-D vs 2-D** is the geometry leftover: nominal Haskell vs OpenSees 2-D.
- **Toro 2022 vs 2-D** uses the same 1-D solver on AR(1)-grid $V_s$ draws. If the geomean stays next to the 1-D nom, randomization is not capturing 2-D shape.
- **Toro ensemble $\sigma_{\ln}$** vs OpenSees spatial $\sigma_{\ln}$ (across recorders) compares each method's own variability estimate.

- **iid** (n=150): OpenSees spatial $\sigma_{\ln}$ 0.131 [0.101, 0.171]; Toro ensemble 0.233 [0.211, 0.244].
- **dipping** (n=144): OpenSees spatial $\sigma_{\ln}$ 0.213 [0.153, 0.262]; Toro ensemble 0.232 [0.222, 0.250].

## Findings

- **iid:** geomean lowers the 1-D peak (Toro 2022 soften-the-peaks); Pearson +0.072, Anderson -0.037 vs 1-D nom. Toro still over-disperses vs 2-D spatial (ensemble 0.233 [0.211, 0.244] vs OpenSees 0.131 [0.101, 0.171]).
- **dipping:** geomean lowers the 1-D peak (Toro 2022 soften-the-peaks); Pearson +0.041, Anderson -0.027 vs 1-D nom. Toro still over-disperses vs 2-D spatial (ensemble 0.232 [0.222, 0.250] vs OpenSees 0.213 [0.153, 0.262]).
